# How Rockets Work

## The Core Principle: Newton's Third Law
Every action has an **equal and opposite reaction**. A rocket pushes hot gas out of its nozzle at very high speed, and the gas pushes the rocket in the opposite direction. That push is called **thrust**.

## The Main Parts
- **Propellant:** fuel plus an oxidizer, because there is no air to burn in space
- **Combustion chamber:** where the propellant burns at extreme temperature and pressure
- **Nozzle:** a bell shape that accelerates the exhaust gas to supersonic speed

## Getting to Orbit
1. The rocket climbs vertically to get out of the thickest part of the atmosphere.
2. It gradually tilts sideways (a "gravity turn"), because orbit is about going sideways fast enough, not just going up.
3. Empty stages are dropped to shed weight, so the remaining engines push less mass.

## Why It's So Hard
The **rocket equation** shows that most of a rocket's launch mass must be propellant: to go faster, you need more fuel, which adds weight, which needs even more fuel.
